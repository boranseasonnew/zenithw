# Why Does ZenithW Exist?

*If cobalt.tools already exists, why did I create ZenithW?*

Well, well, well... :D

First of all, **ZenithW was never created to compete with cobalt.tools.**

In fact, it's quite the opposite.

ZenithW was originally inspired by [cobalt.tools](https://cobalt.tools/). I genuinely liked cobalt. It was clean, simple, straightforward, and did its job without unnecessary complications.

But there was one problem.

**It wasn't quite enough for me.**

I wanted more control, more customization, and more features. I wanted something that could go beyond simply pasting a link and downloading a file.

And that's basically how ZenithW started.

## More Features, Same Simplicity

One thing I always appreciated about cobalt was its simplicity.

I didn't want to lose that.

ZenithW aims to provide more functionality without turning the interface into a confusing mess of buttons, menus, and settings.

**More features shouldn't mean a worse user experience.**

Whether someone just wants to download a video or needs more advanced options, the experience should remain straightforward.

We've also expanded beyond a simple website by developing dedicated applications, including tools designed around YouTube and other supported platforms.

The goal is to give users more freedom over how they download, convert, and manage their media.

And there's still plenty of room to improve.

## What About the Desktop Application?

Starting with [ZenithW Desktop 5.0](https://github.com/boranseasonnew/zenithw/releases/tag/v5.0.0), the Windows app runs on **Rust** and uses Microsoft Edge WebView2 for the interface. The 5.0 installer is about 46% smaller than 4.2 because the app no longer bundles Electron.

Why Rust?

I wanted a lighter Windows package and a core that’s easier to maintain. Rust now handles the queue, file operations and media tools. Video downloads and conversions still depend on the source, your connection and your hardware.

And here's a little confession.

**I'm not going to pretend I'm personally writing every line of code. AI will be doing much of that work.**

I guide the development, make decisions, define the features, and shape what ZenithW should become.

I don't believe using AI makes a project less meaningful. What matters is whether the result actually works, whether problems get fixed, and whether the software is useful to people.

Of course, AI-generated code isn't automatically good code. It still needs testing, review, and improvement.

I'm not interested in pretending otherwise.

## Is ZenithW Trying to Make Money?

**No. That's not why I started this project.**

ZenithW wasn't created to squeeze money out of users.

I don't want to turn basic functionality into a collection of paid subscriptions, artificial limitations, or unnecessary premium tiers.

And I definitely don't want to build a business around selling user data.

**Your data is not the product.**

The goal is to build software that respects its users rather than treating them as something to monetize.

If the project eventually needs financial support to cover infrastructure or development costs, I would rather explore transparent, voluntary ways of supporting it.

But making money has never been the reason ZenithW exists.

## Is ZenithW Better Than cobalt?

That's not really for me to decide.

Cobalt does certain things extremely well, and I respect the people behind it.

ZenithW takes a different approach by offering additional features, more control, and dedicated applications.

Some people will prefer cobalt's minimalism.

Others might prefer ZenithW's flexibility.

**And that's completely fine.**

Not every project needs to defeat another project to justify its existence.

Open-source software should be about giving people choices, sharing ideas, and building better tools.

Competition can encourage improvement, but it doesn't have to become a rivalry.

## What Comes Next?

ZenithW will continue to evolve.

That doesn't necessarily mean adding hundreds of features just because we can.

Sometimes improving performance, fixing a frustrating bug, reducing unnecessary steps, or making an existing feature more reliable matters far more than introducing something new.

I want ZenithW to feel like software that was built because someone genuinely wanted to use it.

Not because a marketing department decided the world needed another subscription.

And I know ZenithW isn't perfect.

There will be bugs, questionable design decisions, and features that need to be reconsidered.

That's part of building software.

What matters is acknowledging those problems instead of pretending they don't exist.

## One Last Thing

I didn't create ZenithW because cobalt was bad.

**I created ZenithW because cobalt was good enough to inspire me, but I had my own ideas about what I wanted next.**

That's really all there is to it.

No grand plan to dominate the market. No secret business strategy. No ambition to become the next billion-dollar tech company.

Just a project that started with curiosity and gradually grew into something bigger.

And honestly?

I'm curious to see how far it can go.

**Built out of curiosity. Improved through experimentation. Made for people, not profit.**

*Thanks for giving ZenithW a chance.* ❤️
