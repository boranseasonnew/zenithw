v7.0 July 2026

the queue finally remembers, and the about tab got a little lighter

Scroll to explore what changed

This might be the update we're personally most relieved about. Previously, if you started a bulk or playlist download and accidentally closed the tab — or hit "stop" and changed your mind — the whole queue reset to zero. Someone who downloaded half of a 40-video playlist and closed the tab had to start all over again. Not anymore.

Alongside that, we also tidied up the "about" section a bit: removed the technical cookie explanation and replaced it with something we think is more meaningful. Let's get into it.

the download queue now picks up where it left off

Progress on bulk and playlist downloads is now saved right in your browser. If you close the tab and come back, or hit "stop" and change your mind, a small notice appears at the top: "you have an unfinished download queue (X/Y completed) — want to continue?" One tap picks up right where you left off; ignore it and it goes away. Videos you already downloaded won't be downloaded again — only what's left gets queued up. This saved state quietly expires after 24 hours, since some platforms' video links stop working after that long anyway.

"stop" is now an actual pause

Stopping the queue used to feel a bit like a one-way decision. Now hitting "stop" is really more of a pause — your progress is saved and you can pick it back up whenever you want, whether that's five minutes or a day later.

removed the cookie explanation from the about section

The "what do we do with cookies?" section was a bit too technical and honestly didn't mean much to most people. In its place there's now an "open source" section explaining that ZenithW's entire codebase is public on GitHub under the MIT license, and anyone can look through it or contribute. Feels like a more concrete way to build trust.

the version number now keeps itself up to date

A small but annoying detail for us: we kept forgetting to manually bump the version number shown in the "about" section (and yes, it did fall behind). It now pulls from a single source, so that won't happen again.

In short: the queue doesn't forget anymore, and neither do we forget the version number.
