v16.0 October 14, 2026

Separate your sound. Keep going with ZenithW.

With v16.0, ZenithW grows into a tool ecosystem for working with your media. The new AI vocal separator prepares vocals and instrumental music on your device. Listen, mix and download the sound you need.

Choose a file; get vocals and music together

We removed the action selection screen. Choosing a file automatically uses the verified UVR-MDX model to prepare both stems in one AI operation. This is genuine model-based source separation. Results vary with the recording.

Two stems, one timeline

Listen to vocals and music separately or together. Waveforms come from the actual audio, with playback position, volume and mute controls. Choose MP3, FLAC or WAV beside each play button, or download both WAV stems in a ZIP.

Your file stays on your device

Separation and export run in your browser; your audio is not uploaded to ZenithW servers. The first run downloads a roughly 67 MB model and runtime assets. Your browser keeps the model cache; storage restrictions, private browsing or low space can prevent persistence.

Less preparation for the next song

Consecutive jobs in the same tab reuse the model and AI session. Memory is released after two idle minutes. Cache loading, AI preparation and track processing have distinct states: every song needs fresh analysis, but the model need not be downloaded again. WebGPU is used when available; CPU processing can be slower.

Tools that work together

Web downloads, conversion and remux tools; Android and Windows apps; the open-source Lilith Audio Engine. All belong to the ZenithW ecosystem. Vocal separation is currently web-only: mono or stereo, files up to 96 MiB and sections up to four minutes. Select a section for longer tracks. Future Android and Windows support for this feature is not guaranteed.

More control over bulk downloads

Duplicate links are removed, selected settings stay fixed during the batch and failed links remain for retry. Limited retries respect server wait instructions. Small-file transfer progress reflects received bytes, keeping preparation distinct from the actual download.
